OSCAR

Create beautiful interfaces to control interactive installations.

Video, lighting and sound, from any screen in the room. Design it in the browser, open it on a tablet, and drive the show over OSC and DMX.

It sends /oscar/hello. Everything on this page sends.

How it works

Download. Design. Control.

No code and no account. OSCAR runs on your own computer, and everything it needs comes with it, so it works in a venue with no internet.

1

Download

Windows, Mac and Linux. Free and open source.

2

Design

Drag controls onto a canvas. Point each at an address.

3

Control

Open it on any device on the network and run the show.

Try them

Every control a show needs

These are not pictures. Each one is a working control you can drag somewhere else, resize, restyle and point at your own software.

Buttons

Momentary or toggle, with the values you choose for on and off.

Faders

Any range, either direction, horizontal or upright. OSC, DMX, or both at once.

XY pads

Two values from one finger: position, pan and tilt, anything with two axes.

Colour

Pick a colour and send it as r, g, b, as a hex code, or straight to an RGB fixture.

Meters

OSC runs both ways. A meter follows what your software sends back.

Send 0 to 1 to /oscar/meter

Exact values

A list to choose from, a number to type, a line of text to send.

Media browser

A wall of clips. One tap launches, and the highlight follows what is playing.

Under the hood

Built for the room, not the demo

OSC, both ways

Send to anything that speaks OSC, and let it answer. Every widget can follow the rig.

Lights, directly

Art-Net and sACN straight from a fader. No lighting desk in between.

Every device agrees

Open the surface on five tablets. Move a fader on one and the rest follow.

Any screen

One layout that rearranges itself for a desk, a tablet and a phone.

Your look

Five widget styles, light and dark, and every colour yours to override.

Pages

A page per room, per scene, per fixture group, with tabs to switch.

Publish and scan

One click gives a page any phone can open. Scan the code and drive.

Arduino and serial

The same messages down a USB cable, to a board you built yourself.
